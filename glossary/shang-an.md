# 上岸 (shàng'àn) — "Reaching the Shore"

**Literal:** climb onto the shore. **Actual:** to get your money back out — break even
or take profit, convert to fiat, and *leave the game*.

## Origin
In mainland slang 上岸 is the universal word for escaping any grinding, precarious
pursuit: passing the civil-service exam after years of cramming, quitting gig work
for a stable job, paying off debt. Its mirror is 下海 (xiàhǎi, "go to sea") — the
1990s term for quitting a state job to do risky business. Crypto adopted the pair
wholesale: you 下海 into the coin circle, and if you're lucky you 上岸.

The embedded metaphor is the confession: **being in the market is being in the
water, drowning.** Nobody says 上岸 about an investment going well; you say it about
an ordeal ending. 永远上不了岸 ("never reaching shore") is the leek's fate — each
回本 (breakeven) tempts one more trade, and the shore recedes.

## Usage
- 回本就上岸 — "the moment I'm even, I'm out" (the lie every [[jiu-cai]] tells)
- 劝人上岸 — group-chat intervention urging a degen to quit
- Contrast 躺平 in [[trading-slang]]: 躺平 is giving up *inside* the water; 上岸 is getting out
