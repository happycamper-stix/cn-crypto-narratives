# Martial & Military Metaphors (战争隐喻)

## Context
Where English crypto slang frames trading as gambling or religion ("degen",
"faith"), Chinese slang frames the market as a battlefield. Much of this register
is inherited from A-share (stock market) retail culture, which itself borrows
from war epics and wuxia fiction.

## Mechanics
- **空军 / 多军 (kōngjūn/duōjūn)** — literally "air force / long army": bears and
  bulls as opposing *armies*. 空 (short) puns on 空军 = actual air force, so bears
  "carpet-bomb" the chart; going short is 做空, going long 做多.
- **埋伏 (máifú, "to lay an ambush")** — accumulate a position quietly before an
  expected catalyst. The trader is a soldier hiding in the grass by the airdrop
  snapshot.
- **冲 (chōng, "charge!")** — to ape in. Battle-cry verb; tripled as 冲冲冲
  ("charge charge charge") in chat spam when a coin starts moving. See
  [[reduplication-cuteness]].
- **打 (dǎ, "to hit/strike")** — the generic combat verb for participating:
  打新 ("hit the new" = snipe new listings/IDOs), 打狗 ("hit dogs" = trade fresh
  memecoin launches), 打金 (play-to-earn grinding), 打板 (limit-up chasing, from
  A-shares).
- **抢筹 / 筹码 (chóumǎ, "chips")** — tokens as ammunition/chips to seize;
  洗筹/吸筹 = shaking out and absorbing supply, framed as siege logistics.
- **护盘 / 砸盘 (hù pán / zá pán)** — "defend the plate" (prop up the price) vs
  "smash the plate" (dump); market-making as fortress defense.
- **解放军来了** — "the liberation army arrives": price recovering to your entry,
  liberating trapped soldiers; a bagholder is 站岗 ("standing guard" at the top)
  or 套牢 (tied up, captured).
- **绝地反击 / 反弹** — last-stand counterattacks and rebounds; every pump is a
  campaign, every crash a rout (踩踏, "stampede").

## Decoding heuristic
If a Chinese trading verb sounds violent or tactical, map the battlefield onto
the order book: armies = long/short factions, ambush = pre-positioning, charging
= market-buying, standing guard = bagholding at the top. The metaphor encodes
*who is fighting whom*, which literal translation loses.
