# Number Slang (数字梗)

## Context
Numbers double as words in Chinese internet culture because digit names sound like
phrases. Tickers, supply counts, and prices are often chosen for these meanings.

## Common values
- **8 / 88 / 888** — 八 (bā) ≈ 发 (fā, "get rich"). The most common lucky digit in
  tickers, supplies, and launch prices.
- **6 / 66 / 666** — 溜/顺 vibes: "smooth, awesome". Praise spam in chats.
- **520** — wǔ'èrlíng ≈ 我爱你 "I love you"; used for launch dates/amounts.
- **1314** — ≈ 一生一世 "for a lifetime" (often paired with 520).
- **4** — 死 (sǐ, "death"): avoided; its absence is itself a signal.
- **9** — 久 (jiǔ, "long-lasting").
- **250** — insult ("idiot"); occasionally used self-mockingly.

## Crypto-specific numbers
- **94 / 九四事件** — Sept 4 2017, the PBoC ICO-ban date. A dated trauma-reference
  every 老韭菜 knows; crashes get named like disasters (312 = Mar 2020, 519 = May 2021).
- **4 inverted** — despite 4≈death, CZ's numbered-tweet shorthand "4" (= "ignore
  FUD") made 4 a bullish meme: hence the Four.meme launchpad and the $4 token.
  See [[launchpad-naming-meta]].
- **YYDS** (永远的神, "eternal god" = GOAT) and the pinyin-initialism family (NSDD,
  XSWL) — letters, not numbers, but same compression culture. 大饼yyds.

## Decoding heuristic
A ticker, total supply, or price full of 8s/6s (or a 520/1314 date) is signaling
luck/affection to a Chinese audience — not a technical parameter.
