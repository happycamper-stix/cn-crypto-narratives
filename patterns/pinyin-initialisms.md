# Pinyin Initialisms (拼音缩写)

## Context
Chinese internet users compress whole phrases into the initial letters of their
pinyin syllables — a habit born partly of typing speed and partly of dodging
keyword filters (a censor scanning for 你妈 won't catch NM). Crypto chats on
Telegram/WeChat/X inherit the entire system, and it's the layer that most reliably
defeats machine translation: the letters look like English but aren't.

## Mechanics
Take each syllable's first letter of a stock phrase:
- **YYDS** — 永远的神 (yǒngyuǎn de shén, "eternal god") = GOAT. `大饼YYDS` = "BTC
  is the GOAT". The single most common praise term for coins that survived a cycle.
- **NSDD** — 你说的对 (nǐ shuō de duì, "you're right") — often dismissive, the
  "sure, whatever" used to brush off FUD or a rival bagholder.
- **XSWL** — 笑死我了 (xiào sǐ wǒ le, "laughing to death") = LMAO; standard
  reaction to rugs happening to someone else.
- **ZQSG** — 真情实感 (zhēnqíng shígǎn, "genuine feelings") — being emotionally
  invested for real, e.g. holding a memecoin not ironically but ZQSG.
- **U1S1** — 有一说一 (yǒu yī shuō yī, "to be fair / honestly") — mixes a digit in:
  "u1s1 这土狗盘子有点东西" ("honestly this shitcoin has something").
- **NB / NiuB** — 牛逼 (niúbī, "awesome", lit. "cow's genitals"); 牛 alone also
  puns on bull market (牛市).
- **SB** — 傻逼, "idiot"; the standard insult for whoever bought your bags.
- **GG** — from gaming ("good game") = it's over, the project is dead.

Crypto-specific extension: **U** = USDT (泰达/U), so `出U` = cash out to stablecoin,
`U本位` = USDT-denominated accounting.

## Decoding heuristic
An all-caps 2–4 letter "word" in a Chinese crypto chat that isn't a ticker is
almost certainly a pinyin initialism. Expand each letter to a pinyin syllable and
look for a stock phrase; digits inside (U1S1, 3Q) are read as their Chinese or
English sounds.
