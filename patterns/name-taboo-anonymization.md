# Name-Taboo & Anonymization (某安 / 孙某 / VX)

## Context
The vault mostly decodes the names people *give* (nicknames, transliterations,
puns). This note decodes the system for *not naming at all* — a three-layer
avoidance convention an analyst must reverse to figure out which exchange or
person a post is actually about:

1. **某X masking** (某 mǒu, "a certain…") for companies/platforms — 某安 = Binance
2. **Legal-register surname masking** (孙某, 赵某鹏) for people — imported from
   police/court style, then parodied by KOLs
3. **Platform-contact evasion** (微信 → VX, Telegram → 纸飞机) for the apps where
   deals actually happen

Unlike [[censorship-evasion-coinage]] (which swaps a sensitive word for an
innocent *object*: 大饼, 糖果), name-taboo keeps a fragment of the real name and
deletes the rest — the reader is meant to reconstruct it instantly. It is
anonymization in form, identification in function: deniability for the writer,
zero ambiguity for the in-group. The companion phrase is 懂的都懂 (dǒng de dōu
dǒng, lit. "those who understand, understand" = iykyk; initialism **DDDD**, see
[[pinyin-initialisms]]).

## Layer 1 — 某X masking of exchanges & platforms

某 (mǒu) is the classical indefinite "a certain / so-and-so", the character
courts and police use for anonymous parties. Prepended to one surviving
character of a brand name, it produces a mask that any 币圈 reader resolves in
milliseconds.

| Mask | Pinyin | Literal | Actually | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 某安 | mǒu ān | "a certain -An" | Binance 币安 | By far the most common; default referent of any unnamed exchange gripe |
| 某OK / 某K (?) | mǒu OK | "a certain OK" | OKX (欧易, ex-OKEx) | Latin letters survive the mask |
| 某易 | mǒu yì | "a certain -Yi" | OKX 欧易 | post-Feb 2021 rebrand form |
| 某火 | mǒu huǒ | "a certain Huo-" | Huobi 火币 / HTX | here the *first* char survives |
| 某币 | mǒu bì | "a certain -coin" | context-dependent; seen for 火币 (?) | ambiguous, needs context |
| 某大所 | mǒu dà suǒ | "a certain big exchange" | usually Binance (?) | 所 clipped from 交易所; sneer register: 某大所又拔网线了 "the big exchange pulled the network cable again" (went down mid-volatility) |
| 某头部交易所 | mǒu tóubù jiāoyìsuǒ | "a certain head/top exchange" | any of the majors | media/formal register |
| 三大所 | sān dà suǒ | "the three big exchanges" | 币安/火币/OK (pre-924 era) | collective form that avoids singling one out |

**Masking rule**: drop the character that is either shared/filterable (币 is a
keyword magnet) or less distinctive, keep the identifying one — 币安 keeps 安,
欧易 keeps 易, 火币 keeps 火. So the analyst's inversion is: take the surviving
character, slot it into the exchange-name inventory, and let context (contract
liquidations → Binance/OKX; 出金 problems → any; BSC memes → Binance) break ties.

The pattern is imported wholesale from the general Chinese internet, where
platforms suppress rivals' names and ad/diversion rules punish brand mentions:
某宝 = Taobao 淘宝, 某乎 = Zhihu 知乎, 某音 = Douyin 抖音, 某东 = JD 京东,
某书 = Xiaohongshu 小红书, 某站 = Bilibili B站. Crypto users arrived already
fluent in it.

**How reflexive it has become**: an October 2024 legal-risk explainer on NetEase
writes the September 14, 2017 exchange shutdowns as 「某易」「某安」「某火」—
masking the names *retroactively, in a historical sentence*, about events the
whole audience can date ([[number-slang]]'s 94). Zhihu answers about liquidation
mechanics likewise discuss "某安's February 2020 insurance-fund change" (?) — a
precisely dated, uniquely attributable fact wearing a fig leaf.

## Layer 2 — Legal-register surname masking (孙某, 赵某鹏)

Chinese police notices (通报 tōngbào), procuratorate case releases, and court
publications mask individuals as surname + 某: 孙某 (Sūn mǒu, "Sun
so-and-so"). Three-character names keep the surname and mask inside: 赵某鹏,
周某凯, or fully 赵某某.

**Canonical crypto example**: the Supreme People's Procuratorate's typical-case
release of December 27, 2023 (virtual currency used for illegal FX exchange)
masks the defendants as 赵某, 赵某鹏, 周某凯, 尤某 — while the media covering it
headline the story as "赵东团队" (the team of Zhao Dong, the famous OTC/RenrenBit
figure). Official register masks; media de-mask in the headline and quote the
masked text in the body. An analyst reading only the official text must do the
same de-masking the journalists did.

Adjacent media formulas for unnamed-but-identified people:

| Form | Pinyin | Literal | Actually |
|---|---|---|---|
| 孙某 / 赵某 | Sūn mǒu / Zhào mǒu | "Sun/Zhao so-and-so" | suspect register; in 币圈 context 孙 defaults to Justin Sun 孙宇晨, 赵 to CZ 赵长鹏 or OTC 赵东 by context |
| 孙姓男子 / X姓富豪 | Sūn xìng nánzǐ | "a man surnamed Sun" | journalism register, pre-conviction caution |
| 孙某人 | Sūn mǒu rén | "a certain person Sun" | KOL sarcasm: mock-legal anonymity where everyone knows it's Justin Sun; the 某 adds contempt, not cover |
| 某不愿透露姓名的孙姓男士 | — | "a certain Mr. Sun who declined to be named" | meme template borrowed from gossip journalism (?); pure 阴阳怪气 (yīnyáng guàiqì, "yin-yang weirdness" = passive-aggressive snark) |
| 某V / 某大V | mǒu (dà) V | "a certain big-V" | unnamed verified influencer, usually mid-accusation of 喊单 — see [[kol-shilling-ecosystem]] |

The sarcastic uses invert the register's purpose: state anonymization protects
the accused; KOL pseudo-anonymization *convicts* them ("孙某人又在营销了" — "that
Sun person is marketing again"). Tone tells you which is which.

## Layer 3 — Platform-contact evasion (VX / ZFB / 纸飞机)

Where Layers 1–2 shield the *writer*, Layer 3 shields the *deal*. Contact info
posted in captions, bios, and OTC ads is written to slip keyword filters and
anti-diversion (引流 yǐnliú, "traffic-pulling") rules:

| Written | For | Pinyin/decode | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| VX / vx / V / 加V / +V | 微信 WeChat | V ≈ wēi sound, X = xìn initial | "why VX not WX" is a standing Zhihu question; both filter-dodge and habit |
| 威 / 薇 / 危信 | 微信 | wēi homophones | 危信 "danger-letter" is the derogatory pun; see [[homophone-wordplay]] |
| ZFB / Z宝 (?) | 支付宝 Alipay | pinyin initials Zhīfùbǎo | payment rail for OTC CNY legs |
| TB / 某宝 | 淘宝 Taobao | — | gift-card/receipt-code laundering contexts |
| TG / 电报 / 纸飞机 / 小飞机 | Telegram | zhǐ fēijī "paper plane" (the logo) | where the group actually lives post-WeChat purges; police anti-fraud notices now gloss 纸飞机 themselves |
| DY / 某音 | 抖音 Douyin | — | — |
| 推 / 蓝鸟 | Twitter/X | lán niǎo "blue bird" | — |

A typical OTC ad reads: 收U，量大价优，+V详谈 ("buying USDT, good price for
volume, add [WeChat] to discuss") — every noun that could trip a filter is
coded. This is the textual surface of the [[dong-ka]] / U商 economy in
[[community-roles]].

**Platform confirmation the practice is mass-scale**: on July 24, 2022 Douyin
published 《关于创作规范表达的说明》 ("Statement on standardized creative
expression") addressing a first batch of 50 high-frequency evasion spellings — creators writing 直播间
(live room) as 波波间/播播间, 福利 (perks) as 浮力 ("buoyancy"), 扫码 (scan the
code) as 扫马 ("sweep the horse"), 划算 as hs, 分享 as fx — and insisting 微信,
淘宝, 钱 were never banned words. The insistence itself documents that users had
concluded, from enforcement behavior, that naming platforms was dangerous.

## Why the names are avoided

- **Censor keywords / survival of the post.** The brands themselves became
  filter terms: Nov 13, 2019 Binance's official Weibo was banned (TRON's the
  same month); Dec 12, 2019 Sun Yuchen's and He Yi's personal Weibo accounts
  followed; June 5, 2021 Weibo mass-banned 30+ crypto KOL accounts (survivors
  stripped 比特币/区块链 from their handles and posed as food/photography
  bloggers); June 9, 2021 Baidu and Weibo blanked search results for
  币安/火币/OKEx (Weibo's notice: "根据相关法律法规和政策，搜索结果未予显示";
  Baidu simply returned no matching pages). Writing 某安
  keeps the post up and the account alive.
- **Libel & retaliation risk.** Justin Sun sued Sina over its 2019–2022
  investigative reports and won in the Beijing Internet Court (ruled Dec 31,
  2024, reported Feb–Mar 2025): deletion of five articles, a 72-hour apology
  on the Sina Finance site, ¥20,000 damages plus ~¥11,000 fees. Binance
  sued the Wall Street Journal for defamation in March 2026. Both principals
  (Sun; He Yi) engage critics directly and litigiously; for a small 自媒体, a
  名誉权 (míngyù quán, "reputation rights") complaint means platform takedown
  long before any court. 某-masking is legally weak — Chinese defamation
  doctrine turns on identifiability (可识别性), and 某安 plus context is
  identifiable (?) — but it defeats the *practical* trigger: brand-name
  complaint sweeps and PR keyword monitoring.
- **Commercial self-preservation.** CN KOL income is exchange 返佣 (fǎnyōng,
  rebate/commission — see [[kol-shilling-ecosystem]]); naming your
  revenue-source exchange in a complaint risks the relationship. 某安 lets a KOL
  vent to insiders without surfacing in the exchange's brand-search dashboard.
- **Register inheritance.** A decade of police 通报 and court releases trained
  readers that 孙某 *is how serious accusations are written*. Using the form
  borrows its gravity (or parodies it).
- **Search de-indexing as feature.** A masked post is invisible to outsiders
  searching the brand but perfectly findable by insiders searching the mask —
  soft access control on top of evasion (?).

## Decoding heuristics for the analyst

1. 某 + character: match the surviving character against the exchange/platform
   inventory; 安→Binance, 易/OK→OKX, 火→Huobi. Default unnamed-exchange referent
   in complaint posts is Binance (largest CN user base, see
   [[binance-gravity-well]]).
2. 某 + surname context: in 币圈, bare 孙某/孙某人 ≈ Justin Sun; 赵某 needs
   disambiguation (CZ vs 赵东 vs an actual case defendant — check whether the
   register is sarcastic KOL-speak or quoted 通报 text).
3. Official documents mask, media headlines de-mask: to identify parties in a
   procuratorate/court release, find the news coverage of the same case.
4. Tone test: protective anonymity is flat and consistent; contemptuous
   pseudo-anonymity co-occurs with 阴阳怪气 markers (呵呵, 懂的都懂, "不点名了").
5. Provenance signal: Taiwan/HK outlets (BlockTempo etc.) freely print 幣安 and
   孫宇晨 — dense 某-masking in a Chinese-language text marks a mainland writer
   or mainland-platform distribution; see [[hk-tw-mainland-divergence]].
6. Layer-3 strings (VX, ZFB, 纸飞机, +V, 收U) in a post are a strong signal of
   OTC/solicitation content, and of scam risk when combined with 带单 language.
