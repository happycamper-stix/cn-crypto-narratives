# Kinship & Household-Hierarchy Metaphors (亲属称谓, qīnshǔ chēngwèi)

## Context
Chinese kinship terminology is a ranking technology. Unlike English ("uncle",
"aunt", "cousin"), the Chinese system grammaticalizes four dimensions in every
term: **generation**, **birth order** (大/二/三…), **paternal vs maternal line**
(叔 vs 舅, 姑 vs 姨), and **blood vs marriage vs adoption** (亲 vs 表 vs 干).
A single one- or two-character address is therefore a coordinate in a rank
lattice — and 币圈 discourse recruits the whole lattice wholesale. Coins get
slotted into an old-style polygamous household (旧式大家庭) with BTC as
patriarch; people get addressed with kinship rank terms that encode standing,
intimacy, and — via one homophone swap — moral verdict. The register is as
systematic as [[food-metaphors]] or [[martial-military-metaphors]], but where
food encodes *censorship camouflage* and war encodes *who fights whom*, kinship
encodes *who outranks whom and who is legitimate*.

The generative loop: a homophone seeds a kinship reading (以太 → 姨太, see
[[homophone-wordplay]]), and then the kinship system's built-in genealogy
**auto-completes the rest of the family for free** — every fork, rival, and
backer inherits a pre-made slot and title. No other register self-extends this
cheaply.

## The coin household (imperial-family schema)

| Term | Pinyin | Literal | Actual | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[da-bing\|大饼]] / 老大 / 大哥 | dàbǐng / lǎodà / dàgē | big flatbread / eldest / big brother | BTC | patriarch of the household; "老大-老二" hierarchy framing is explicit in CN explainers |
| 二饼 / 二哥 | èrbǐng / èrgē | second flatbread / second brother | ETH | birth-order prefix = market-cap rank; 三饼/四饼 exist but never stabilized |
| [[da-yi-tai\|姨太 / 大姨太]] | yítài / dà yítài | concubine / first concubine | ETH | homophone of 以太; affinal + female-coded = *not in BTC's bloodline*, joined the household "by marriage" |
| 二姨太 / 小姨太 | èr yítài / xiǎo yítài | second / junior concubine | ETC | ETH's own fork ranks as a *junior concubine*, not a prince — forks inherit the parent's gender/legitimacy coding; both numberings attested (?) |
| 太子 / 大太子 | tàizǐ / dà tàizǐ | crown prince | BCH | Aug 2017 fork of BTC = son of the patriarch with a succession claim ("the real Bitcoin") |
| 二太子 / 小太子 | èr tàizǐ / xiǎo tàizǐ | second / junior prince | BSV | Nov 2018 fork *of the fork* = second in the line of succession; genealogy auto-generates the title |
| 大姨妈 | dàyímā | eldest maternal aunt (slang: menstrual period) | IOTA (?) | MIOTA ≈ sound of 大姨妈 (?); maternal-line term = an in-law visitor, not household core; the period pun mocks its recurring crashes (?) |
| 亲儿子 | qīn'érzi | biological son | exchange-built chain | BSC = 币安亲儿子; SOL was FTX's 亲儿子 (FTX picked Solana for Serum in July 2020, held ~55.8M SOL, >10% of supply); Base = Coinbase 亲儿子 (?) |
| 干儿子 | gān'érzi | adopted/god-son | invested-in (not built) project | the 亲/干 blood-vs-adoption distinction maps build-vs-back relationships (?) |
| 干爹 | gāndiē | godfather / sugar daddy | a project's powerful backer | "这项目有干爹" = it has an exchange/VC patron who won't let it die (?) |
| X之父 | zhī fù | father of X | founder | 比特币之父 中本聪, 莱特币之父 李启威 — the respectable public-register version of the same schema |

**Reading the schema:** patrilineal titles (太子) carry *legitimacy claims* —
BCH's whole marketing was succession. Affinal female titles (姨太) deny
legitimacy while conceding intimacy with power — ETH is *in* the household but
can never inherit the BTC throne. Birth-order prefixes are a live market-cap
league table. The 亲/干 distinction prices the strength of an exchange's
commitment to a chain. The one metaphor system encodes protocol genealogy,
market rank, and legitimacy politics simultaneously — a literal translation
("Auntie ETH"?) loses all three.

The 孙哥→孙割 flip is the register's most important mechanic: **the address
term is the verdict**. Chinese speakers must choose *some* title to refer to a
person, so the choice itself broadcasts the community's judgment — and a
one-character homophone substitution ([[homophone-wordplay]]) lets the verdict
hide inside the honorific's own sound.

| 二舅币 | èrjiù bì | Second Uncle Coin (SUC) | late-Jul 2022 rug pull (launched Jul 28, −99.7% by Jul 30) | minted days after the viral July 2022 Bilibili video 《回村三天，二舅治好了我的精神内耗》; ~US$1.3M taken; copycats 大舅币, 二舅妈币, 草根二舅币, 二爷币 — kinship terms as ticker-bait |

The pattern: **manufactured kinship manufactures trust**, and every major CN
crypto scam runs on it. The [[dai-dan-lao-shi]] group chat is a staged
household — teacher, assistants, "fellow students" who are all staff. 家人们 is
the tell: nobody calling you family in a token group is your family.

## The state and platforms as parents

| Term | Pinyin | Literal | Actual | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 央妈 | Yāngmā | Central Mother | People's Bank of China | standard CN finance slang (mother who controls the household money and bails out the children); in crypto discourse the parent who administered the 94 (2017) and 924 (2021) spankings — see [[censorship-evasion-coinage]] |
| 财爸 | Cáibà | Finance Dad | Ministry of Finance | 央妈's counterpart in mainland fintwit |
| 币安爸爸 / 交易所爸爸 | Bì'ān bàba | Daddy Binance / daddy exchange | listing gatekeeper | follows the 马云爸爸 template: the platform you depend on is your father; getting listed = winning the father's favor ([[binance-gravity-well]]) |

Regulation-as-parenting and platform-as-father complete the household: retail
(韭菜/家人们) at the bottom, KOL 老师s and 大佬s above them, exchange 爸爸s
above those, and 央妈 over everyone.

## Decoding heuristic
When a coin or person carries a kinship term, read four signals:
1. **Birth-order prefix** (大/二/小) = market-cap or seniority rank, live-updated.
2. **Patriline vs affinal/maternal** = legitimacy: 太子 claims succession;
   姨太/姨妈 marks an outsider inside the household; 舅舅 marks unverifiable.
3. **亲 vs 干** = built-by vs merely-backed-by.
4. **Register shifts are verdicts**: honorific→homophone (哥→割) is conviction;
   kinship→deification (神) is canonization; sudden 家人们 intimacy from a
   stranger is an incoming rug.
